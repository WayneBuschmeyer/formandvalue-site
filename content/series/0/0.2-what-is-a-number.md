## Introduction

People are often taught that numbers represent quantities. Three apples. Five dollars. Twelve people. In that view, a number stands for a collection of objects, something we can count.

But numbers are not limited to counting. There is another way of seeing them, one that begins not with quantity, but with space.

In this view, a number is a distance. It describes movement from one point to another. That movement might represent time, temperature, weight, or something more abstract. But whatever the context, a number tells you where you are in relation to where you began.

From this, everything else unfolds.

---

## What Is a Number

Let us begin with a single point in space. Then we choose a second point and place it somewhere else. The distance between these two points becomes our unit.

One movement. One step. One of something.

If we take three of those same steps, we reach what we call **3**. Each step adds one more copy of the unit distance.

We can write this as:

Start ⟶ Unit Distance = 1

Start ⟶ 3 Unit Distances = 3


But we could reverse the order. Instead of moving from the first point to the second, we begin at the second and return to the first.

Same distance. Flipped direction. That becomes **–1**:


The positive and negative values are not different in kind. They are the same structure, just read in different directions.

This is what it means to say that –1 is the reverse of 1. Not a separate idea, but a flipped relationship.
